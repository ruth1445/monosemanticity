Path 04 · The payoff

Steering &
Safety

The untangling trick worked on a toy. In 2024, Anthropic pointed it at Claude 3 Sonnet — a production frontier model — and found millions of features, a way to steer with them, and the first real glimpse of a safety dashboard for a mind made of numbers.

01It scales

From 512 neurons to 34 million features

The open question after Path 03: does dictionary learning survive a jump of several orders of magnitude? Anthropic trained sparse autoencoders of three sizes — 1M, 4M, and 34M features — on the middle layer of Claude 3 Sonnet, using scaling laws to pick the training recipe. It worked.

And the features got deeper. One fires for the Golden Gate Bridge — in English, Japanese, Korean, Russian… and in photos of it, despite the SAE training on text alone. Features track concepts, not words. Nearby features cluster by meaning too: around the bridge sit Alcatraz, the 1906 earthquake, Ghirardelli Square — a genuine map of concepts.

A feature that fires for the idea of a thing — across languages and even across senses — is exactly what "concept" was supposed to mean all along.

03Why it matters

The features you'd want an alarm on

The bridge is the fun demo. The serious result is what else turned up in the dictionary: features for security vulnerabilities and backdoors in code, bias and slurs, lying and deception, sycophancy (telling users what they want to hear), power-seeking, and dangerous content. If a model's concerning cognition is legible as features, you can hope to monitor it — notice when "deception" lights up during an answer — and eventually intervene.

04Honest limits

What this hasn't solved

Good science says the quiet parts out loud — these caveats are from the papers themselves:

01Partial coverage. SAE reconstructions aren't perfect, and 34M features is likely far fewer than the model actually uses. Some of the model's mind stays off-dictionary.
02Cost. Capturing every feature of a frontier model could cost more compute than training the model itself. Efficiency is an open research problem.
03Finding ≠ understanding. A feature tells you what the model represents, not how it uses it. Tracing the circuits that connect features into reasoning is the sequel — Anthropic's attribution-graph work traces actual multi-step computations through the model.

The arc you just walked: thoughts are points → distance and direction carry meaning → too many concepts, so the model superposes them → sparse autoencoders untangle it → millions of real, steerable concepts — including the ones safety depends on. That's the state of the art, and it's barely five years old.
